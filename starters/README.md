# Starter models — pick one and you are running

You do not need a theory of anything to start. You need one belief you already
hold, written down before reality settles it.

Each starter below is a whole model: the belief, what would prove it wrong, and
the first claim to register. Copy one, change the specifics to your life, and
you have a working instrument by the end of the evening.

**If you only take three, take these:** My Own Predictions (B1), The Thing I Keep
Being Wrong About (B3), and Who Actually Decides (I2). The first teaches the
loop, the second finds your blind spot, the third is the one people say changed
how they see their job.

---

## BASIC — one belief, one date, no jargon

You are learning the loop: write it down, wait, find out.

### B1 · My Own Predictions
**The question you already ask:** "I think this is going to happen — am I right?"

Model anything you already have opinions about: your team's deadline, a
election, whether the restaurant survives the year. One belief, one date, one
way to tell. That is the entire model.

*First claim:* something you believe about the next 90 days that a friend could
score without asking you what you meant.

### B2 · The Prices I Notice
**The question:** "Is this actually getting more expensive, or does it just feel
that way?"

Pick five things you buy — coffee, rent, a streaming service, petrol, one
grocery item. Predict each one's direction and rough size over six months.
Feelings about prices are famously unreliable; this is the cheapest way to find
out whether yours are.

*First claim:* "By [date], [thing] will cost more than [amount]."

### B3 · The Thing I Keep Being Wrong About
**The question:** "Where does my judgment keep failing?"

Most people have one. You think meetings will be short. You think the project
will land on time. You think the family argument is settled. Name yours, then
predict the next three instances — this is the model most likely to teach you
something uncomfortable.

*First claim:* the next specific instance, with a date.

### B4 · What My Friends Will Care About
**The question:** "What will people be talking about next month?"

Predict which topics reach your circle and which die quietly. Simple, oddly
hard, and it makes the difference between "interesting" and "spreads" visible.

*First claim:* "By [date], at least three people I know will mention [topic]
unprompted."

## INTERMEDIATE — a mechanism, not just a guess

Now you say *why*, and the why is what gets graded.

### I1 · Where The Money Comes From
**The question:** "Who pays for this, and what happens when they stop?"

For an organisation you care about — employer, club, local paper, favourite
café — trace the actual revenue. Then predict what changes when the largest
source wobbles. Most surprises are visible in the funding structure first.

*First claim:* a specific consequence of a specific funding change, dated.

### I2 · Who Actually Decides
**The question:** "Who really makes this call?"

The org chart and the decision-maker are rarely the same person. Model one
decision that affects you — a policy at work, a planning application, a school
rule — and name who genuinely decides, who only appears to, and what they each
need. Then predict the outcome.

*First claim:* what the next decision will be, and by whom.

### I3 · What They Cannot Say
**The question:** "What is the thing nobody in this situation is allowed to
mention?"

Every organisation has constraints it cannot state publicly. Predict behaviour
from the constraint rather than the announcement — this is where reading the
press release gets you the wrong answer.

*First claim:* an action that the stated reasons do not predict but the
unstated constraint does.

### I4 · The Bill Coming Due
**The question:** "What maintenance is being skipped, and when does it bite?"

Deferred repairs, unhired roles, ignored technical debt, the friendship nobody
is tending. Things that work until suddenly they do not. Name the deferral and
predict when the cost lands.

*First claim:* the specific failure and its rough window.

### I5 · Same Story, Two Sides
**The question:** "Why do both sides think they are obviously right?"

Take a dispute you have a view on. Write the strongest version of the side you
disagree with — not the strawman, the version its smartest advocate would sign.
Then predict which framing wins the retelling.

*First claim:* which vocabulary neutral people use in three months.

## ADVANCED — models that check other models

You have graded claims now. These read your own record.

### A1 · Am I Calibrated?
**The question:** "When I say 70%, am I right 70% of the time?"

Not "am I right often" but "does my confidence mean anything". Almost everyone
is overconfident in a stable, measurable way, and once measured it can be
corrected.

*First claim:* a prediction about your own hit rate in your next ten claims.

### A2 · Right For The Wrong Reason
**The question:** "Did my reasoning work, or did I get lucky?"

For each past hit, ask whether the mechanism you named actually operated. Hits
and lucky hits feel identical from the inside, which is exactly why this needs
measuring rather than remembering.

*First claim:* what fraction of your next ten hits will be earned.

### A3 · The Boring Explanation
**The question:** "What if it is just incompetence, timing, or luck?"

Build the simplest sufficient account of things you have explained with
mechanisms — no strategy, no plan, nobody clever. Bet on it against your own
theories. Boring wins more often than anyone expects.

*First claim:* pick one of your own explanations and bet the boring version.

### A4 · What I Am Not Watching
**The question:** "The thing that decides this — am I even looking at it?"

For a situation you follow closely, list what you monitor, then list what could
resolve it that you are *not* monitoring. Write that second list down before
the outcome, because afterwards it is worthless.

*First claim:* the resolution arrives from something on the unwatched list.

### A5 · What Changed My Mind
**The question:** "What did I believe a year ago, and what moved me?"

Track your own revisions: what you thought, what changed it, whether the change
held. This is the model that tells you whether you are learning or drifting.

*First claim:* a prediction about which current belief you will abandon first.

---

## Choosing

- **Never done this?** B1 and B3. Two claims, ninety days, and you will know
  more about your own judgment than most people learn in a career.
- **Work in an organisation?** I1 and I2 pay for themselves fast.
- **Ten graded claims already?** A1 and A2 — the advanced tier needs a record
  to read, and is not worth building before you have one.

One honest warning: the advanced models can be discouraging. Most people find
their honest hit rate is roughly half what they assumed, and most early hits
turn out to be lucky rather than earned. That is the point, and it is the part
that makes the rest worth anything.
